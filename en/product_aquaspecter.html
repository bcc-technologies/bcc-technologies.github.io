How it works

From water to data, in three steps

Impedance spectroscopy applies a small alternating electrical signal to the sample and measures how it responds across a frequency sweep. That response describes the sample without altering it.

The sample

The electrode contacts the liquid sample. The frequency sweep, drawn as the bars of the drop, runs through the sample from high to low frequencies.

The signal

AquaSpecter applies a small AC perturbation and records magnitude and phase at each frequency.

The data

The response becomes Bode and Nyquist curves ready to interpret and report, with the same criteria every time.

Two ways to measure

What you get

Curves ready to interpret

Every measurement produces the two standard EIS views, in the same format from one sample to the next.

  • Nyquist: separates solution resistance, charge transfer and diffusion.
  • Bode: shows magnitude and phase against frequency to compare samples at a glance.
  • Output ready for reporting and for equivalent-circuit fitting with EIS-Toolkit.

Illustrative curves from a Randles circuit with Warburg diffusion. Not AquaSpecter measurements.
